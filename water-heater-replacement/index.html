When a failed water heater is an emergency

A tank pouring water across the floor, or a gas heater that has gone cold and smells wrong, cannot wait. Our line is open 24/7, and we book same-day appointments when available. While you wait, close the cold water valve above the heater, switch off its power or gas control, and keep everyone out of standing water near outlets.

Water heater replacement in Wasco, CA means taking out a failed or worn-out tank and setting a new unit on the home's existing water, gas or electric connections. Most tanks warn first. Rust-colored hot water, a rumble or pop from inside the tank, and damp insulation at the base all point toward the end.

The key question is where the water is coming from. A leak at a fitting or valve can often be fixed, but a leak through the steel shell of the tank cannot. Once the shell has corroded through, no patch holds, and replacement is the only real repair.

Shut off the supply and the power: do this first

These steps limit damage until the plumber arrives. Skip any step that puts you near water and electricity at the same time.

Before the plumber arrives

  • !Close the cold supply valve Turn the handle above the heater clockwise until it stops. If it will not move or the leak continues, close the main water valve at the house instead.
  • !Cut power to an electric heater Switch off the heater's breaker, but only if you can reach the panel without stepping through water. If the floor is wet around the panel, leave it and wait for help.
  • !Handle a gas heater with care If it is dry and safe, turn the gas control to off. If you smell gas, leave the building on foot right away, then call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 or 911 from outside, and never turn the gas back on yourself.
  • !Do not drain a hot tank by hand Water inside a tank that was heating can scald. Let it cool, and leave draining to the plumber.
  • !Photograph the label The data plate shows capacity, fuel type and model. Send those details when you call so the replacement can be matched before the visit.

From the old tank out to the first hot tap

A straightforward swap is a matter of hours. Here is the order the work follows.

  1. Match the new unitCapacity, fuel type, vent size and the space around the old heater decide what fits. The plumber checks these against the label before ordering or pulling stock.
  2. Isolate and drainWater, power and gas are shut off at their sources. The tank is drained to a floor drain or outdoors, with a hose run so nothing floods the garage.
  3. Disconnect and removeSupply lines, gas or electric connections and the vent come off. Old flex connectors, shutoff valves and fittings are inspected rather than reused by default.
  4. Set, brace and connectThe new tank sits level on its pad or pan and is strapped for earthquake movement. New connectors go on, plus a fresh relief valve discharge pipe.
  5. Fill and purge airThe tank fills with a hot tap open upstairs, so trapped air escapes. Power or gas stays off until the tank is full.
  6. Light or energize, then testGas joints get a leak check, the vent is confirmed to draw, the thermostat is set, and the plumber walks you through the shutoff valves.

Gas, electric or heat pump: choosing the new unit

The simplest swap keeps the same fuel. A gas tank replaced with a gas tank reuses the gas line and vent, and an electric tank replaced with an electric tank reuses the circuit. Changing fuel means new gas piping, venting or electrical work, which widens the scope and the price.

A heat pump water heater moves heat from the surrounding air into the tank instead of making heat directly, so it needs open air around it and space to drain condensate. The 2025 Energy Code, in effect since January 1, 2026, encourages heat pump water heaters in new homes. It is not a flat rule for replacements, and gas water heating in new dwellings must reserve space and provisions for a future heat pump unit.

Wasco summers matter to this choice. Bakersfield, the nearest National Weather Service climate station, averages a July high near 98 F. A heat pump unit also cools the air around it, which some owners welcome in a hot garage and others find a drawback in a small closet. For a tankless alternative, gas line capacity and venting become the main questions instead.

Bracing, relief valve and expansion tank

California law requires water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake movement (Health and Safety Code 19211), and that applies to replacement units. The temperature and pressure relief valve is the tank's last safety device, so its discharge pipe must stay open and uncapped.

A thermal expansion tank gives heated water somewhere to go when the system is closed. Whether your plumbing needs one depends on the setup, and the plumber checks. Where water pressure is above 80 psi, the California Plumbing Code calls for a pressure regulator, so a pressure reading is part of a proper visit.
