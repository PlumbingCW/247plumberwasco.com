Do these five things while you wait for the plumber

What you do in the first few minutes often decides how much of the house gets wet. None of this needs tools.

Stop the damage first

  • !Close the main water valve Look where the line enters the house or by the meter box. Turning it off also keeps the water in the heater and toilet tanks from draining out.
  • !Stop using drains If a drain is backing up, pause the dishwasher, washer, showers and toilets. Every gallon you add looks for the lowest opening.
  • !Keep power away from water Stay out of wet areas with outlets, cords or the heater's electrical connection. Switch off a breaker only if you can reach it dry.
  • !Smell gas? Leave first Walk out on foot, then call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 or 911 from outside. Do not call a plumber before you are clear.
  • !Call the 24/7 line Say what you see, where the main valve is and your cross street. That is enough for dispatch to line up the right plumber.

Where Wasco's water comes from, and what that means at home

The City of Wasco gets its water from groundwater wells in the Kern County subbasin. The city's 2022 water quality report counts six active wells and about 94 miles of distribution lines, and a chlorine residual averaging 0.67 ppm. A faint chlorine smell from the tap is therefore normal, and a carbon filter at the faucet is one fix.

That report does not list hardness, so we do not quote a number. Check the tap with a test strip or lab test before buying a softener, and read the newest city report for anything about water quality. We do not judge health questions, and a plumber cannot treat the city supply.

The report also describes the city's plan to drill new wells and treat an existing one. Because that work changes what comes out of the tap, read the latest version on the City of Wasco site rather than relying on an older copy.

Whose pipe is it? Sewer, leaks and backflow under Wasco code

Under the city's sewer code, your house connection runs from 12 inches outside the building wall to the city main, and maintenance is the property owner's responsibility. A backup between the house and the main is therefore yours to repair, and a sewer line repair may be the answer if clearing the line does not hold.

The code also requires a cleanout where the house line meets the soil pipe. No permit is needed to clear a stoppage or repair a leak, but replacing the sewer with a different material needs one. Ask your plumber to confirm before digging.

Wasco's water conservation chapter expects leaks to be fixed, and it treats 48 hours after discovery as reasonable. A running toilet or sprinkler leak is worth fixing soon. Irrigation and fire-line owners should also know the city's backflow chapter calls for assemblies to be tested at least annually by a state-certified tester.

Soil, slabs and hidden leaks around Wasco

The USDA describes the Wasco soil series as a well-drained sandy loam on alluvial fans, found mainly in the southern San Joaquin Valley. Water that escapes from a buried line can soak away instead of pooling, so a hidden leak may show up first as a high bill or a damp patch near the meter. Our leak detection page explains how acoustic and thermal tools pinpoint such leaks without opening the whole yard.

The state classifies the Kern County Subbasin as critically overdrafted, and falling groundwater can cause land to sink. We cannot say that subsidence is breaking Wasco pipes, because no source we found links the two. A pipe that leaks should still be found and fixed, whatever the cause. According to EPA guidelines, reading your water meter, using no water for two hours and reading it again shows whether a leak is likely.

Permits, licenses and who to call at City Hall

City Hall is at 746 8th Street. Wasco has no separate Building department in its directory, so start with Community Development or Planning when you need a permit answer. The state plumbing code in effect since January 1, 2026 is the 2025 edition, while the city's adopted code text still names the 2022 edition, so confirm with the city before a heater swap or repipe.

California requires a contractor license for plumbing work above a small dollar threshold set by the state, and a license can still be required on small jobs that need permits or workers. According to the Contractors State License Board, you can verify any plumber's license online. Verify first, whoever you hire.
