Hot water: four pages for four different situations

A tank that has quit or started to leak is a water heater replacement conversation more often than a repair one. According to the Department of Energy, storage water heaters last 10 to 15 years, so age tilts the decision. A heater with a bad element or thermostat but a sound tank belongs on the repair page instead.

Planned swaps and first-time installs have their own page for water heater installation, where venting, gas connections and bracing are the main topics. California law requires water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake movement, and that applies to replacements. If you are leaning toward endless hot water, a tankless unit is worth reading about, since DOE says these last about 20 years but need regular descaling.

Slow drains, sewage and the lines beneath the house

Start with the simplest tool that fits. A single clogged sink or tub usually needs drain cleaning, and a line that clogs again within weeks may call for hydro jetting to clear grease and scale from the pipe walls. A camera is how you learn which one you have.

Trouble in the main line is a different job. A cracked or collapsed pipe needs sewer line repair, and recurring roots or buildup point to sewer line cleaning. If you are buying a house or have had repeated backups, a sewer inspection shows the condition before you commit to a repair. In Wasco the owner is responsible for the house connection to the city main, so knowing the pipe's condition matters.

Leaks you can see and leaks you cannot

A spreading ceiling stain or a wet wall often means a supply or drain fault inside the framing, and finding it without opening everything is the point of leak detection. According to EPA guidelines, a meter test is a good first check: read the meter, use no water for two hours, and read it again. If the reading changed, you probably have a leak.

When a pipe splits outright, burst pipe repair is the right page. Warm floors or the sound of running water under a slab point to slab leak repair, while a soggy yard near the meter or sudden low pressure suggests the service line between meter and house. Homes with pipes that keep failing may be better served by a repipe than by another patch.

Fixtures, disposals and new plumbing

The small jobs are the ones people put off, and they cost more when they wait. A running or leaking toilet wastes water the whole time, a dripping faucet wears out its seat, and a jammed or leaking garbage disposal can soak the cabinet below.

New lines, added fixtures and remodel rough-ins are covered under plumbing installation. Where static water pressure runs above 80 psi, the California Plumbing Code calls for a pressure regulator, which is worth raising during any new install.

Gas, water treatment and business sites

Gas work is safety first. If you smell gas, leave the building on foot and call SoCalGas or 911 from outside, then see gas line repair for what a plumber can do once the utility has cleared the area. According to the Contractors State License Board, the C-36 plumbing classification covers gas appliance connections and gas piping from the owner's side of the meter to the structure.

Water that leaves scale or tastes of chlorine leads many homeowners to softener and filter installation. Because mineral levels differ between Wasco, Shafter and McFarland, test your own tap first. Shops, restaurants and rental properties have their own needs, from after-hours leaks to grease traps and backflow assemblies, which are handled on the commercial plumbing page.
