Emergency Slab Leak Repair in Wasco, CA

A floor that feels warm for no reason, flooring that lifts at the seams, or a meter that spins while every wall stays dry can all trace back to a pipe leaking in or under a concrete slab.

This page covers how those leaks are pinpointed and the three ways they get repaired.

Water under the floor? Shut it off first

Water that surfaces through a concrete floor is an emergency because it keeps flowing under pressure all day. Our line is open 24/7, and we send a licensed plumber the same day when one is available. Until they arrive, close the main valve, keep appliances and cords off the wet floor, and stay off any warm or slick area.

A slab leak is a leak in a water pipe that runs inside or beneath a poured concrete foundation. This applies to slab-on-grade homes, and not every home in Wasco, CA is built that way, since some sit over a crawl space. If yours is, the leak gets pinpointed before any concrete is touched.

What a slab leak feels and sounds like

The giveaway is often a warm patch on the floor, especially where hot water pipes pass beneath. A leaking cold line is quieter, showing up as damp carpet edges, lifting laminate, baseboards that swell or a musty smell at the base of walls. A meter that runs while every fixture is shut tells the same story from the outside.

At night you may hear a faint rush or hiss when the house is quiet. A water heater that cycles far more than usual may also point to hot water escaping before it reaches a tap. If the symptoms only show up in one room, tell the plumber which one.

How the leak is pinpointed before concrete is touched

A good locate saves the most money on a slab job. This is the general sequence a plumber follows.

  1. Confirm it is a supply leakA meter test and a pressure check on the home's water lines rule out a toilet, a faucet or a drain problem. According to EPA guidance, you read the meter before and after two hours with no water in use, and a changed reading probably means a leak.
  2. Isolate sections of pipeValves and temporary caps split the system into runs. The run that loses pressure holds the leak.
  3. Listen and traceAcoustic tools pick up leak noise through the slab, and pipe location equipment follows the route of the line under the floor. Tracer gas and thermal imaging are also used to confirm a location, so ask which methods your plumber used.
  4. Confirm with a second methodThermal imaging of a warm spot, or tracer gas introduced into an emptied line, can back up the first result before anyone cuts.
  5. Mark the spot and explain the optionsYou see where the leak is and which repair path fits, along with a clear price before work starts.

Reroute, epoxy lining or dig down: comparing slab leak repairs

There are three common paths. The best one depends on how many leaks exist, what the pipe is made of and how much of the house you want disturbed.

OptionWhat it involvesUsually fits whenTrade-off
Reroute the lineNew pipe runs through walls or the attic, and the old line is cappedSeveral leaks, aging pipe, or a long run under the floorMore wall and ceiling patching, but no concrete breaking
Epoxy pipe liningA coating is applied inside the existing pipe to seal pinholes and weak spotsPipe is structurally sound but thinning, and access points are availableNot suited to every pipe, and does not fix a split or a bad fitting
Under-slab spot repairConcrete is cut over the leak, the pipe section is replaced and the floor is patchedOne clear leak in an otherwise healthy lineNoise, dust, floor patch and finish matching

Ask your plumber to explain why one option beats the others for your pipe, not just which one costs least up front. If other leaks appear later, a reroute can look better in hindsight, and the plumber will tell you how worn the rest of the pipe looks.

Hot line or cold line, and why it changes the repair

A hot water leak under a slab often heats the floor surface above it, so it is easier to feel and to find with thermal tools. A cold line leak is cooler, slower to spot and more likely to be found by listening and pressure tests. Either one can undermine flooring long before the meter shows a big jump.

If the plumber finds the pipe is worn along its whole length, patching one spot may only buy time before the next leak. In that case a repipe of the affected run can be the cleaner answer. If you are still unsure where the water is going, hidden leak detection comes first.
