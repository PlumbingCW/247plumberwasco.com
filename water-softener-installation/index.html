Water Softener & Filtration in Wasco, CA

A water softener installation in Wasco, CA connects a treatment tank to your main supply line so hardness minerals are removed before the water reaches your fixtures.

Softeners, carbon filters and reverse osmosis units solve different problems, and the right pick starts with a test of your own tap.

Crusty faucets, cloudy glasses and chlorine taste are three different problems

White crust on faucet tips and showerheads, spots on dishes and a film in the tub usually point to hardness minerals. A chlorine smell or taste is a separate issue, and so is a flat or off taste at the kitchen sink. Mixing these up is how people end up with a tank they did not need.

A plumber can tell which problem you have by looking at the fixtures, the water heater and a simple test of the water. That is why the first call is about what you see, and the equipment comes second.

Softener, filter or reverse osmosis: what each one is for

These are not interchangeable. Pick by the problem you want gone.

OptionWhat it doesWhat it does not doWhere it goes
Salt-based softenerRemoves hardness minerals that cause scale and spottingDoes not work as a general filter or fix taste on its ownAt the main line, whole house
Carbon filterReduces chlorine taste and odorDoes not soften waterWhole house or at one tap
Reverse osmosis unitTreats drinking and cooking water through a membraneSlow, small volume, not meant for the whole houseUnder the kitchen sink
Combination setupSoftener for scale plus a tap-level filter for tasteCosts more and has more parts to serviceMain line plus sink

Most households need one of these, not all four. If you are unsure, describe what you notice on the call and the plumber can match the equipment to the complaint.

Test your tap before you buy anything

Hardness is easy to measure and expensive to guess. Test strips from a hardware store give a rough number, and a lab test gives a precise one. Take the sample from a cold tap that has not been softened.

Then compare your result with your city's annual water quality report. The result tells you how big a tank you need, and whether you need one at all.

Wasco, Shafter and McFarland publish very different hardness numbers

The City of Wasco's 2022 water quality report does not list hardness, so a Wasco homeowner has no city number to start from. Test the tap, and check the city's newer reports for hardness before sizing equipment.

The neighbors report it, and the gap is wide. Shafter's 2025 report gives total hardness of 92.17 ppm on average, with individual wells from 17 to 207 ppm, while its 2024 report averaged 128.85 ppm. McFarland's 2025 report lists hardness of 11 ppm, with a range of 7 to 32.4 ppm.

That means a softener that makes sense for one address in Shafter may be pointless in McFarland. Wells can differ from one part of a city to another, so your own test beats the citywide average.

Ask your city about salt discharge

California has no statewide ban on salt-based softeners. According to California's Water Code, a local agency that runs a community sewer system may regulate residential self-regenerating softeners, but only after a regional water board finding and a public hearing, and the options include barring new units.

The law names the San Joaquin River and Tulare Lake regions among those covered. We have not confirmed whether any local agency in Wasco, Shafter or McFarland has adopted such a rule, so ask your city before installing a self-regenerating softener.

What an installation actually involves

The work is mostly plumbing, and the details decide whether the unit works quietly for years.

  1. Pick the spotA garage wall, side yard or utility closet near the main line, with room for the tank and brine tank and a nearby electrical outlet.
  2. Cut into the mainThe plumber shuts the water, cuts into the supply line and sets inlet, outlet and a bypass valve so the water can be routed around the tank for service.
  3. Route the drainThe unit sends rinse water to a drain through an air gap, so the line must reach an approved drain and be secured.
  4. Check pressureWhere static pressure runs above 80 psi, the California Plumbing Code calls for a regulator, and high pressure is hard on tank valves.
  5. Set and testThe head is programmed for your hardness, the system is flushed, and the plumber checks fittings for drips before leaving.

Reverse osmosis and what the Wasco report lists

People often ask about reverse osmosis after reading the city's water quality report. The city's 2022 report lists nitrate and 1,2,3-TCP results and lists 1,2,3-TCP as a violation, and the city describes plans for new wells and carbon treatment at one existing well. Read that report and call the city's water utility for current results.

A softener is built for hardness minerals, not for those. We install the equipment you choose, but we do not diagnose water-quality health risks or promise that a tap unit removes a given contaminant. If that is your goal, check what a product is certified to remove before you buy.

A softener changes how the rest of the plumbing is set up

Where the tank connects matters for the water heater. Soft water flows to the heater and fixtures, so many installers plan the tank ahead of the heater and keep outdoor hose bibs on the untreated line. If you are also thinking about a new water heater, planning both jobs together saves a second cut into the main.

Scale also stays on old fixtures until it wears off. Expect crusty aerators to need a soak or replacement, and do not expect spots to vanish the day after install. Other new fixtures and lines are covered under plumbing installation.

Existing softener or conditioner that has stopped working

A tank that no longer regenerates, a salt bridge, a cracked valve from a move or a leaking brine line are common reasons for a service call. Tell dispatch the make and what you notice on the control head.

If the unit is leaking, turn the bypass valve and call. The work can be handled as plumbing under the C-36 plumbing classification, which includes water conditioning equipment. You can check any contractor's license at the CSLB website.
