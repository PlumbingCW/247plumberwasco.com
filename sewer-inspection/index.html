Sewer Line Inspection in Wasco, CA

Most sewer problems stay invisible until water backs up, and by then the easiest moment to learn about them has passed.

A sewer line inspection in Wasco, CA is a camera run through the pipe between your house and the city main. A small waterproof camera on a flexible rod shows the pipe's condition live and records it.

Seeing inside the pipe before the pipe decides for you

A camera inspection is a planned visit, not a rescue. You schedule it because you want facts: what is the pipe made of, where does it bend, is anything growing into it, and does it hold water where it should run downhill. Sewer pipe is buried, and without a camera the only clues are slow drains and a hunch.

The camera rides on a flexible rod fed in through a cleanout, which Wasco's code requires where the house sewer meets the soil pipe. The plumber watches the live picture and records it. You get an answer that you can show to a seller, an insurer or a landlord.

Five reasons people book a camera run

The reasons below cover almost every inspection we schedule. Pick the one that sounds like yours.

  • ✓Buying a home A pipe that fails after closing is yours to repair. A look before you commit lets you negotiate or walk away.
  • ✓Backups that keep returning If the same line has been cleared two or three times, repeating the clearing without looking is guesswork.
  • ✓Finding where the line runs Before a patio, addition or tree planting, you need to know the route and approximate depth of the pipe.
  • ✓A slow drain with no obvious cause When several fixtures are sluggish but nothing has fully stopped, the camera finds the narrowing or the sag.
  • ✓Before and after other work Footage taken before cleaning or lining, and again afterward, shows what the work actually changed.

Home buyers: what to ask before you close

A general home inspection usually looks at what is above ground. The buried sewer line may not be included, so ask whether it was checked. In Wasco the lateral from 12 inches outside the building wall to the city main belongs to the property owner, which means a defect found after closing is a repair you pay for.

If you are buying in Shafter or McFarland, note that those city pages do not state who owns the lateral, so ask that city's Public Works what applies.

Request the video, not just a verbal summary. Your agent and the seller can then see the same pipe you saw, and any repair estimate can be tied to a specific point on the footage.

Repeated backups: look before you clear again

Clearing a line removes the symptom. It does not tell you why the line keeps stopping. A camera run answers the question by showing whether the cause is grease, roots, a bellied pipe or a broken joint.

The answer shapes the fix. Grease and scale point toward hydro jetting followed by better habits. Roots and cracks point toward a repair. A line that is sound but heavily used may only need a scheduled cleaning.

According to the Oro Loma Sanitary District, a California utility, a homeowner with a persistent clog should hire a licensed plumber to inspect the line by camera and ask for a copy of the results and video. That is good advice anywhere.

Locating the line, the cleanout and the slope

A camera with a locating transmitter lets the plumber trace the pipe from above ground and mark it with paint or flags, along with an approximate depth. That is useful before a fence, a slab or a new tree goes in. It is not the same as the utility mark-out you request before digging, and you should still call 811 before any excavation.

Slope matters because drain lines rely on gravity. California plumbing code expects horizontal drain pipe to slope at least 1/4 inch per foot, and the City of Wasco sets a minimum fall of 1 foot in 50 feet for house connection sewers. When the camera shows standing water along a stretch, the slope there may have been lost.

What a good inspection report should include

Ask for these items before you pay. A report that lacks them is not much use in a negotiation or an insurance claim.

  • ✓Recorded video The full run from cleanout to the end of the accessible line, in a format you can keep and share.
  • ✓Pipe material and approximate size Cast iron, clay, plastic or fiber pipe each fail differently.
  • ✓Distance marks for each finding A defect at a known footage can be located and repaired. A vague note cannot.
  • ✓Plain-language description What each finding means, whether it needs action now and what the options are.
  • ✓Limits of the inspection If the camera could not pass a blockage or a bend, the report should say how far it went.

What a camera inspection cannot tell you

A camera sees inside the pipe. It does not see soil conditions outside, and it cannot prove a pipe will last a given number of years. A joint that looks tight today can shift later.

Debris, water or a heavy bend can stop the camera short of the main. In that case a cleaning pass or a second entry point may be needed, and the report should say so honestly. Treat the footage as a snapshot of the day it was recorded.
