Water on the floor at closing time, or a drain that will not clear

A business plumbing problem rarely waits for office hours, and our 24/7 emergency line takes the call whenever it happens. While you wait, close the main water valve if a pipe is spraying or pooling, stop running sinks and dishwashers if a drain is backing up, and keep staff and customers out of any wet area near outlets, coolers or panels.

Take photos of the damage and the source before you move anything. They help later with the property owner and with an insurance claim, if one applies.

Commercial emergency: the first ten minutes

These steps protect people and stock while a licensed plumber is being lined up. None of them needs tools.

Do this before you call back or leave

  • !Find the main water shutoff Look near where the water line enters the building, often by the meter box or a utility room. Closing it also saves the water sitting in tanks and heaters from draining out.
  • !Stop adding water to a bad drain Turn off dishwashers, ice machines and mop sinks that share the blocked line. Every flush or rinse sends more water toward the lowest drain in the building.
  • !Keep power and wet floors apart If water is near panels, cooler compressors or floor outlets, stay clear and let the electrician or utility deal with that side. Move food and stock to dry shelves only if the floor is safe.
  • !Smell gas? Leave first A rotten-egg smell is a different emergency. Walk out, then call SoCalGas or 911 from outside. Our gas line repair page explains the order of calls.
  • !Tell dispatch what kind of building it is Say whether you own the building or lease it, whether the problem is a leak, a drain or a backup, and whether customers are on site. That tells us how to scope the visit.

Restaurants, shops, farm and packing operations, and offices

Each kind of business breaks pipes in its own way. Restaurant and bakery drains carry fat, flour and food solids, so repeat backups usually mean a trap or a coated line, not a one-time accident. Retail strips and offices tend to have restroom groups on shared branch lines, which is why one blocked toilet can show up as a gurgling floor drain two units away.

Farm and packing facilities have wash-down drains, hose bibs, irrigation connections and sinks that run hard in season. In that kind of building the useful question is whether a recurring problem sits on the building's own piping or comes from the line out to the street. Our dispatch asks before anyone is sent, so the right plumber arrives with the right job in mind.

Drains that keep clogging in a business building

A floor drain or restroom line that keeps stopping up has usually been cleared and left alone. A licensed plumber can run a cable through it, and for stubborn fat or scale hydro jetting cleans the pipe wall instead of poking a hole through the blockage.

Camera work shows whether the real problem is a belly, a root intrusion or a broken joint. If the line itself needs drain cleaning regularly, ask for a camera look so the next visit is a repair plan, not another rodding.

Commercial leak detection when the bill and the building disagree

A water bill that climbs while nothing looks wet is the classic hidden-leak sign. The EPA's meter test works in a business too: shut off everything you can, read the meter, wait two hours with no water in use, and read it again. If the reading moved, water is going somewhere.

From there, the visit narrows it down with acoustic and thermal methods and, where needed, leak detection at the suspect line. The City of Wasco's conservation code requires leaks to be fixed and treats 48 hours after discovery as reasonable, so a confirmed leak should not sit.

Grease trap cleaning

Grease traps and interceptors catch fat before it reaches the sewer, and a full one is behind a lot of kitchen backups. Cleaning means removing the trap contents and scraping the baffles, and in many cases that is a hauler's job rather than a plumber's.

Tell the line what kind of trap you have and where it sits. We will say plainly whether we can handle the service or whether you need a grease hauler, and a plumber will still deal with the drain line behind it. For any discharge rules on grease, ask the City of Wasco's public works office, since we have not confirmed local requirements.

Backflow testing and the city's water code

A backflow assembly keeps water from your irrigation, fire line or process piping from flowing back into the public supply. The City of Wasco's code puts the cost of installing a required assembly on the property user, calls for testing at least annually and again after installation, relocation or repair, and says the test must be done by a state-certified tester.

According to the State Water Board, backflow assembly testers must be certified by a recognized or accredited organization. Ask your tester for proof of certification before you pay, since the city can reject a test from someone who is not certified.

It also lets the city enforce this by ending water service for refusal. So if you got a notice, or an inspection is due, call and say so. Where an assembly is required, annual testing by a certified tester applies, and a plumber can repair or replace a failed device.

Storm drain cleaning on private property

Parking lots, loading areas and wash pads often have private catch basins that fill with silt, leaves and trash. When one clogs, water ponds at the building edge and can find its way inside.

Clearing a private drain line is routine plumbing work, but what happens downstream may belong to the city or another agency. The visit can clear your side and tell you where responsibility changes, so you know who to call about the rest.
