Wasco: where the line is based

Wasco has a single ZIP code, 93280, and the business address on State Route 46 sits inside it. The city's sewer code treats the house connection from about a foot outside the building wall to the city main as the property owner's responsibility. So a backup that starts between your house and the main is usually your repair, and a camera look is the first step toward knowing where it is.

That ownership rule is why sewer line repair and a camera inspection come up so often on Wasco calls. Ask the city before assuming a stoppage in the street line is yours, since the main itself belongs to the city.

Shafter: reaching it along State Route 43

State Route 43 connects Wasco and Shafter, a drive of about 8 miles. Shafter's ZIP is 93263, and the Shafter page covers how its housing and water shape the calls we take there.

Two local rules are worth knowing. The Shafter building permits page lists water heaters among its permit types, so a swap there is a permitted job to plan for. The city's plumbing code also bars water piping inside a concrete slab on grade, with exceptions, which affects how a repipe gets routed in slab homes.

McFarland: Highway 99 splits the town

Highway 99 runs through McFarland and, according to the city's history page, divides it into east and west sides. When you call, say which side of the highway you are on and give a cross street. The McFarland page covers ZIP 93250 and its local details.

The city lists water heater replacements among the projects you can apply for through its online permit system. If a tank is failing, replacement should include a conversation about that permit.

Water is not the same three miles apart

Shafter's 2025 report lists average hardness of about 92 ppm, with individual wells ranging from 17 to 207 ppm. McFarland's recent reports show soft water, and Wasco's 2022 report does not list hardness at all. A decision about a softener or filter therefore starts with a test of your own tap, not with a number from a neighboring city.

If your question is about what is in the water for drinking, read your city's annual water quality report and contact the utility. A plumber installs equipment, but we do not diagnose water quality health risks.

A note on Delano

Delano has no page on this site and we are not listing it as a covered town. If you live there, call the line, give the address, and ask whether a visit is possible. You will get a straight answer rather than a promise.
