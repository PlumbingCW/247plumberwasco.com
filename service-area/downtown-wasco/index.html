Where Downtown Wasco sits, and where we are guessing

Local guides describe the small downtown district along 7th Street as Wasco's shopping and dining center. No city map we could open draws a boundary around it, so this page uses anchor points instead of lines.

The anchors are City Hall at 746 8th Street, the Wasco Union High School Auditorium at 1900 7th Street, the Amtrak platform at 700 G Street and the Adventist Health medical office at 1040 7th Street.

If your address is a few blocks off 7th or 8th Street, say so when you call and we will tell you plainly if this page fits. Homes farther out along a long road like Palm Avenue have their own page, Plumber on Palm Avenue.

The auditorium was completed in 1929 and is on the National Register of Historic Places, so the area has older civic buildings. That says nothing about the pipes inside any particular home or shop. We ask about your building instead of guessing from the neighborhood.

Whose pipe is it? What Wasco code says about the sewer line

According to Wasco's municipal code, a house connection sewer is not city property. It runs from 12 inches outside the building wall to the city main, and maintenance and repair are the property owner's responsibility.

That matters on a block where buildings sit close together. Before you pay for a main-line job, find out whether your building has its own connection or shares one. We cannot see that from the phone, but the owner or property manager often knows.

The same code requires a cleanout where the house connection meets the soil pipe. Clearing a stoppage needs no permit, but altering or replacing a connection with a different material does, and the permit comes from the public works director. The Public Works line in the city directory is (661) 758-7271. For a blocked line that needs drain cleaning, a cleanout is where the work usually starts.

Backflow assemblies are a business job, not a drain job

The city code on cross-connections says the water user installs a required backflow assembly at their own expense. The assembly must be tested at least once a year and after any installation, relocation or repair, and only a state-certified tester can do it.

A shop or restaurant that receives a notice about an assembly has a different problem from a clogged sink. Tell us which one you have when you call, so the right conversation happens first. Our commercial plumbing page lists what we can talk through for businesses.
