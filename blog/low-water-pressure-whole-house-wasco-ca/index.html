Whole-House Low Water Pressure: Where the Problem Usually Sits

When water pressure is low at every tap, the cause is almost never one faucet. It is usually a shutoff valve that is not fully open, a failing pressure regulator, a leak or corrosion in the line from the meter, or aging pipe inside the house.

A simple gauge on an outdoor spigot and a few quick checks tell you which of those you are dealing with, and whether the city should be called instead of a plumber.

Quick checks that cost nothing

Run these before you pay for a visit. Several take less than a minute.

  • ✓Test several taps If only one tap is weak, clean its aerator. If every tap is weak, the cause is upstream.
  • ✓Compare hot and cold Low pressure only on the hot side points to the water heater or its shutoff valve, not the whole house.
  • ✓Ask the neighbors If the next house has the same weak flow, the problem may be on the supply side.
  • ✓Open the main shutoff fully A gate valve left a quarter closed after past work cuts the flow to everything. Turn it fully open.
  • ✓Watch the meter With all water off, a moving dial means water is escaping. That can steal pressure from the house.

If the meter is moving, the EPA's two-hour meter test is the next step, and a plumber can locate the loss without digging at random.

What a pressure gauge tells you

A plumber measures static pressure with a gauge screwed onto an outdoor hose bib. You can do the same with a hardware store gauge. Read it with every fixture off, then open a tap and watch the needle fall.

As a yardstick, one California utility, San Diego Public Utilities, says a new pressure-reducing valve should be set between 45 and 75 psi. The California Plumbing Code calls for a regulator where static pressure is above 80 psi, with an expansion tank in the cold water piping after it.

If the gauge reads healthy at rest but drops hard when a tap opens, the line is restricted. If it reads low even at rest, the regulator or the supply side is the likelier cause. According to EPA guidance, a water meter reading that changes over two hours with nothing running points to a leak, which can also explain weak pressure.

When the pressure regulator is the culprit

A pressure regulator is a bell-shaped valve near the main shutoff in many homes. It wears out, and a failing one can restrict flow, let pressure creep too high or make a humming noise. Not every house has one, and the gauge reading is the clue.

Replacing a regulator is work for a licensed plumber. Because the code pairs a regulator with an expansion tank in the cold water piping, the plumber may suggest checking that tank while the water is off.

When the cause may be on the City of Wasco's side

The City of Wasco's 2022 water quality report says the city supplies groundwater from six active wells through about 94 miles of distribution lines. The city has also gone through environmental approval for four new wells to replace existing ones, and a treatment project at Well 12.

That work is the utility's business, but it is a reason to ask the city first if your whole street seems weak. If neighbors report the same drop, call the City of Wasco water utility before paying for a diagnostic visit at your own house.

We do not claim any of that work causes low pressure in a given home. If the neighbors are fine and your meter checks out, the problem is probably on your side of the meter.

Old pipe and slow decline

Galvanized steel supply pipe corrodes from the inside, and the bore narrows over the years. One plumbing reference puts its typical life at 40 to 60 years, with copper lasting much longer in most conditions. A house plumbed in galvanized often shows weak flow that gets worse slowly, along with rusty water on first draw.

We do not know the pipe materials in Wasco homes from the city's data, so a camera or an access cut is the only way to confirm. If an interior pipe is the problem, repiping replaces the narrowed lines, and a plumber can scope the walls and access points by phone first.

A plumber can also test the line from the meter. If the loss is in the buried service line, the fix is a service line repair, not a whole-house job.
