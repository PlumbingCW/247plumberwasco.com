How to Install a TOTO Toilet in a Wasco Home

A TOTO toilet swap is a one-afternoon job when the old toilet comes out cleanly and the floor underneath is sound. The trouble nearly always hides under the old bowl, not in the new one.

By the Wasco 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 29, 2026. Updated October 29, 2026.

Call (661) 338-1180 now

Can you install a TOTO toilet yourself?

Yes, a careful homeowner can install a TOTO toilet in Wasco. The work is a swap: shut off the water, empty and lift out the old toilet, inspect the drain flange, set the new bowl on a fresh wax ring, tighten it down evenly, connect the supply line and test for leaks. The new fixture rarely causes problems. What you find under the old one does.

Plan on a second person for lifting, because porcelain is heavy and chips easily. If you uncover a cracked flange, soft flooring or a shutoff valve that will not close, stop there. A licensed plumber can repair those in the same visit and then set the toilet.

Check these before you open the box

Most failed toilet installs trace back to something that could have been measured or tested the day before. Walk through this list with the old toilet still in place.

  • ✓Rough-in distance. Measure from the finished wall behind the tank to the center of the floor flange bolts. The model you buy has to match that measurement, so read the spec sheet for the exact model number, not just the product line.
  • ✓Bowl shape and clearance. An elongated bowl reaches farther into the room than a round one. Check the swing of the door and the distance to the vanity before you commit.
  • ✓Power for a Washlet seat. A bidet seat needs electricity near the toilet. If there is no outlet in the right place, that is an electrician's job, and it can change which model makes sense.
  • ✓The shutoff valve. Close the valve under the old tank and open the flush. If the valve spins freely, weeps or will not stop the flow, replace it first. Do not build a new install on a valve you cannot trust.
  • ✓Flush volume. California's CALGreen sets toilets at 1.28 gallons per flush. A model sold for California should meet that, and the box or spec sheet will say so.
  • ✓The floor. Press around the base of the old toilet with your palm. Spongy flooring or a stain on the ceiling below points to a slow leak that has been soaking the subfloor.

If any of these checks fails, you have learned it before any damage was done. Fixing the problem first is almost always simpler than discovering it with a new toilet half set.

The swap, in the order that works

Lay down towels, wear gloves and keep a bucket and sponge close. The sequence below assumes a standard floor-mounted two-piece or one-piece toilet.

  1. Shut off the water and empty the toiletClose the supply valve, flush, and hold the handle until the tank stops filling. Sponge out the last water from the tank and bowl so it does not slosh when you lift.
  2. Disconnect the supply lineLoosen the nut at the bottom of the tank with a towel under it, since a little water will run out. Remove the nuts at the base caps, then rock the toilet gently to break the seal.
  3. Lift out the old toilet and scrape the flangeCarry it to a plastic sheet. Scrape off the old wax from the flange, and stuff a rag into the drain opening so sewer gas stays out of the room while you work.
  4. Inspect the flange and closet boltsThe flange should sit solid on the finished floor with no cracks or rust gaps. Replace the closet bolts with new ones and slide them into the slots with the threads up.
  5. Set the wax ring and lower the bowlRemove the rag, place a new wax ring, then lower the bowl straight down over the bolts. Press down with your weight to compress the ring, and do not rock the toilet side to side.
  6. Tighten in small turns, then attach the tankAlternate between the two nuts a little at a time so the porcelain seats evenly. Over-tightening cracks the base. For a two-piece model, follow the installation guide for the tank bolts and gasket.
  7. Connect the supply, open the valve and testHand-tighten the line, open the valve slowly and let the tank fill. Flush several times and run a dry paper towel under every joint to catch a weep you cannot see.

Snags you can fix and snags that need a plumber

Use this table when something under the old toilet does not look right. When in doubt, a plumber's visit costs less than a ruined subfloor.

A rocking toilet should never be fixed by tightening harder. That cracks the base and breaks the seal.

Wasco note: a weeping connection counts as wasted water

The City of Wasco's water conservation chapter requires leaks to be fixed, and it presumes 48 hours after discovery is a reasonable time to do it. A drip at a new supply connection or around the base of a freshly set toilet falls under that same idea of preventing waste.

In practice, check every joint again the next morning and again after a day of normal use. If a connection still weeps after a gentle quarter-turn, shut the valve and have it looked at rather than waiting.

When a plain swap turns into a repair

A leaking base, a toilet that keeps running or a bowl that slowly loses water are repair problems, and the part you choose should match the fault. Our page on toilet repair explains how these faults are found, and the plumber we send can often fix them without replacing the toilet at all.

If you are moving the toilet, adding a bathroom or running a new supply line, the scope changes from a swap to new plumbing. That kind of job belongs under plumbing installation, where fixture locations, venting and permits all matter.

California requires a contractor license for work worth the CSLB threshold in combined labor and materials. Under that amount a license can still be required when the job needs permits or employees, so check any plumber at cslb.ca.gov before work begins.
