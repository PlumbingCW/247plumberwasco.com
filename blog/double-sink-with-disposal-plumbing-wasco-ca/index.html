How a Double Kitchen Sink With a Disposal Is Plumbed

A double kitchen sink with a garbage disposal drains through one shared trap. The disposal sits under one basin, and its outlet joins the second basin's drain through a waste tee before everything flows into a single trap and out to the wall.

The details below show each part, the order of assembly, and where the dishwasher fits in.

By the Wasco 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 29, 2026. Updated October 29, 2026.

Think of the setup as three short pipe runs that merge. The disposal outlet runs sideways, the second basin drops straight down from its strainer, and both meet at a tee. From the tee a single tailpiece drops to the trap, and the trap arm carries the water into the wall.

Sharing one trap is normal. Two traps in a row on the same drain, sometimes called double trapping, can make a sink gurgle and drain slowly. If you find that arrangement under your sink, it is worth having someone look at it.

Follow the water: how the drain path goes together

Install order matters, since the pieces nest into each other. Work from the sink down.

  1. Set the strainersSeat the flange on basin 2 with putty or a gasket and tighten it from below. On basin 1, the disposal mount takes the place of a standard strainer.
  2. Hang the disposalLift it onto the mounting ring and lock it in place. Turn the power off at the breaker first, and confirm the disposal is dead before touching the wiring.
  3. Connect the two drainsRun the disposal outlet to the tee and the basin 2 tailpiece to the same tee. Point the disposal side slightly downward so water does not pool.
  4. Drop to the trapAttach one tailpiece below the tee, then the trap, then the trap arm to the wall stub. The trap arm should slope toward the wall, since the plumbing code's general rule for horizontal drain pipes is a fall of at least 1/4 inch per foot.
  5. Add the dishwasher drainRemove the knockout plug in the disposal inlet, then clamp the drain hose to it. Forgetting the plug is a classic reason a new dishwasher will not drain.
  6. Test for leaksFill both basins, release them, and run the disposal. Dry each joint with a paper towel and check again after a few minutes.

Dishwasher drain: high loop or air gap

The dishwasher hose should rise to the underside of the counter before it drops to the disposal inlet, forming a high loop. This keeps sink water from running back into the dishwasher. Some homes use an air gap fitting on the counter instead, so follow whichever your installation already has.

Why the second basin drains slowly

A common complaint on double sinks is that one basin drains fine while the other lags. When the disposal side is slower, grease buildup in the tee or trap is the usual cause. When the plain basin is slower, the tee may be installed with too little downward slope.

A plumber clears the trap first and then checks that the trap arm falls toward the wall. If the line behind the wall is partly clogged, drain cleaning with a cable clears it from the cleanout or the trap opening. Routine care, such as running cold water while the disposal works, keeps grease moving down the line.

Shopping list for a new double-sink drain

Take a photo of your current setup and bring this list to the store.

  • ✓Waste tee or continuous waste kit Matched to the pipe size under your sink, which is usually printed on the old fittings.
  • ✓Trap and trap arm Choose the same material as the rest of the drain, so the connectors seal cleanly.
  • ✓Slip-joint washers A fresh set of gaskets for every connection is the best protection against leaks.
  • ✓Plumber's putty or gasket For the basin 2 flange, depending on the strainer you buy.
  • ✓Dishwasher drain hose clamp Needed when a new dishwasher is hooked up to the disposal.
  • ✓A bucket and rags Old traps hold dirty water, and you will spill some.

When to hire it out

Swapping a tee and trap is a manageable weekend task. Moving the sink, adding a second disposal or opening a wall to change the drain slope is not. California requires a contractor license for work worth one thousand dollars or more in labor and materials, so check any plumber at cslb.ca.gov.
