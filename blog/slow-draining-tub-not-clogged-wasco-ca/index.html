Why a Bathtub Drains Slowly When Nothing Looks Clogged

A tub that drains slowly with no visible clog usually has hair and soap film packed below the stopper, a stopper that does not fully lift, or a partial blockage further down a shared line.

A few checks in about ten minutes sort out which one it is, and they tell you whether this is a stopper job or a plumber job.

By the Wasco 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 29, 2026. Updated October 29, 2026.

Standing water around your ankles in the shower is easy to blame on a clog you cannot see. Most of the time the blockage is real but hidden: a mat of hair and soap sitting just below the drain opening, on the stopper mechanism, or in the trap under the floor.

Less often the tub is the messenger and the real trouble sits in a shared branch line or in the vent that lets air into the system. That is why the first move is to look at what else in the house is slow, not to reach for a chemical bottle.

Ten minutes of checks before you buy anything

Start with the simplest and safest tests. None of these needs more than a screwdriver and a flashlight.

  1. Watch how the water leavesFill a few inches and pull the stopper. A slow, steady drop points to a partial clog. Gurgling or bubbling in the tub or a nearby sink points to air trouble.
  2. Lift the stopper out and clean itMany tub stoppers unscrew or pull straight up. Hair wraps around the stem and the underside, and it narrows the opening every time the tub is used.
  3. Check that the stopper really opensOn a lever-style tub, the linkage behind the overflow plate raises the plug. If it has slipped, the plug sits half closed even when the lever says open.
  4. Run other fixtures nearbyTurn on the bathroom sink and flush the toilet while watching the tub. If the tub bubbles or water rises, the blockage is farther down the line than the tub trap.
  5. Ask which fixtures are slowOne slow tub is a local problem. Several slow drains across the house point to the main line, which is a different job.

What the pattern usually points to

The same slow tub can come from different causes, and the pattern around it is the best clue.

What you seeLikely causeNext move
Only the tub is slow, no soundsHair and soap film at the stopper or in the trapClean the stopper, then cable the drain
Tub stays slow after stopper cleaningFilm further down the tub lineCable or snake the line, or call for drain cleaning
Gurgling when the sink or toilet runsShared branch partly blocked or a vent problemStop DIY and have the line scoped
Tub and floor drain slow togetherMain sewer line starting to restrictCall soon, before it backs up
Cleaning helps for a day, then it slows againDrain slope too flat or a sagPlumber evaluation

The last row is the least common and the hardest to fix with cleaning. A drain that holds water because of its slope has a layout problem, not just buildup.

Why a tub clogs differently from a kitchen sink

A kitchen drain collects grease and food. A tub drain collects hair, body oil and soap, which bind together into a gray film that coats the pipe wall and keeps narrowing the opening. Because the film is thin and soft, you rarely see a lump to pull out.

A tub also drains through a trap set in the floor, with a fairly flat run behind it. Flat runs move water slowly, so a thin layer of buildup has a bigger effect than it would on a steep pipe.

The California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), generally calls for horizontal drain pipes to slope at least 1/4 inch per foot. If your tub line was built flatter than that, or has settled, it will keep slowing down no matter how often you clean it.

Think twice about chemical drain openers

A bottle poured into a slow tub often just sits above the blockage, and the next person to open the trap or run a cable meets caustic water. If a chemical was used, tell the plumber before the visit.

Hard water and soap scum in Wasco: what the city report does and does not say

The City of Wasco's 2022 water quality report says the supply comes from groundwater wells in the Kern County subbasin, but it does not list hardness. So there is no city figure to tell you whether mineral scale is helping soap film build up in your drain.

A home hardness test strip is a quick way to check your own tap. If the result is high, the soap scum story is more likely, and a softener or different soap may help over time.

Hard water or not, the first fixes stay the same: clean the stopper, clear the line, check the slope. For water quality questions, read the city's annual report or ask the water utility. We do not diagnose water quality.

When a plumber is the right call

Call when more than one fixture is slow, when you hear gurgling, or when the tub drains normally for a day after you clean it and then slows again. Those patterns suggest the blockage is past the tub trap, where a hand tool cannot reach.

A drain cleaning visit uses a cable or similar tool to scour the film from the tub line, and a camera can confirm the line is clear afterward. If several drains share the problem, main sewer line cleaning may be the real job.

Keeping the tub drain moving

A hair catcher over the opening stops most of the problem before it starts, and costs very little. Pull the stopper and wipe the hair from it on a regular schedule, since the mat grows every week.

Pour hot tap water down the drain after a long soak to move soap further along the pipe. Skip the habit of washing pet hair or heavy grease through the tub.
