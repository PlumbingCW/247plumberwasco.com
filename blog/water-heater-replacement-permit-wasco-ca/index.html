Do You Need a Permit to Replace a Water Heater in Wasco?

In most California cities, replacing a water heater needs a permit and a final inspection. Wasco's own rule is not posted where we could verify it, so the safe move is to ask the city before the old tank comes out.

The straight answer for a Wasco homeowner

Replacing a water heater usually needs a permit and inspection from your city or county building department. That is the general rule across California, and it applies whether the new unit is a standard tank or a tankless water heater. What we cannot confirm is the exact Wasco procedure, because the city pages we could open do not state it.

So treat it as a permit job until the city tells you otherwise. A licensed plumber doing a water heater replacement normally handles the permit as part of the work, and you should ask who is pulling it before you sign anything.

How a permitted replacement usually runs

The details differ by city, but the sequence is similar everywhere a permit applies.

  1. Confirm who filesAsk the plumber whether they apply for the permit and whether the fee is part of the quote. In McFarland, contractors also need a city business license and insurance on file.
  2. Apply before the work startsA permit is easier to get before the tank is removed. Starting first can force an awkward conversation with the city, and an inspector may ask to see work that is already closed up.
  3. Swap the heaterThe plumber installs the new unit with the required straps, the discharge pipe for the relief valve, and whatever shutoffs and connections the code asks for.
  4. Schedule the inspectionThe inspector checks the finished installation. Keep the area open and the heater accessible until the city signs off.
  5. Keep the paperworkA signed-off permit is a record that the work was inspected. It is useful if you sell the house or file an insurance claim for water damage later.

What the inspection tends to look at

Inspectors work from the code the city has adopted. These items come up because state law or the plumbing code speaks to them.

  • ✓Earthquake bracing. California law requires water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake movement (Health and Safety Code 19211). Replacement units are covered, not just new homes.
  • ✓Pressure and expansion. Where static water pressure is above 80 psi, the California Plumbing Code calls for a pressure regulator and an expansion tank on the cold line after it. A pressure gauge on a hose bib tells you where your house stands.
  • ✓Relief valve discharge. City handouts elsewhere in California describe the relief valve drain as piped to a safe location, full size and not capped or trapped. Ask your plumber to show you where yours ends.
  • ✓Gas and venting. On a gas unit, the connection, the shutoff and the flue are checked. A gas leak response is a separate matter: leave the building, then call SoCalGas or 911 from outside.

A reliable plumber will explain these before the visit, not leave you to find out from the inspector.

Wasco note: the city's code and the state's code are on different editions

The 2025 California Plumbing Code took effect statewide on January 1, 2026. The City of Wasco's municipal code, in the version we found, adopts the 2022 edition of the plumbing code by reference and shows no local plumbing amendments.

For you, that means the edition on the inspector's desk may not match what a contractor read last month. Ask Community Development or Planning at City Hall which edition the city is enforcing, and whether it wants a permit for your specific replacement.

Why the license matters as much as the permit

California requires a contractor license for work worth the CSLB threshold in labor and materials, and the C-36 plumbing classification covers equipment installed to heat water. A water heater job almost always crosses that threshold, so check the plumber at cslb.ca.gov before you pay a deposit.

CSLB's guidance on down payments is a small share of the job capped at a set amount, so be cautious about anyone asking for much more up front. Pulling a permit is part of doing the work properly, and an unlicensed installer is the person least likely to want an inspector involved.

Heat pump water heaters and the 2025 Energy Code

The 2025 Energy Code, in effect since January 1, 2026, encourages heat pump water heaters in new homes. It is not a flat rule that every replacement must be a heat pump, so a standard tank or tankless swap is still on the table. Ask the plumber what the city expects for your house.

Permit questions homeowners ask

Some cities allow owner-builders to apply, and McFarland asks owner-builders to sign an acknowledgment form. A gas heater has real safety risks, and the work must meet code whoever does it. Check with the city and consider a licensed plumber.
