What Decides the Price of Finding a Hidden Leak

A leak detection quote in Wasco depends mostly on where the leak might be and how hard it is to reach. A leak under a bathroom sink and a leak under a slab are very different searches.

By the Wasco 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 29, 2026. Updated October 29, 2026.

The short answer on leak detection pricing

Leak detection is priced on the work of finding the leak, not on repairing it. The main drivers are how many lines have to be tested, how accessible they are, which tools the search needs and how long it takes. We do not publish a price list because a fair number only exists after someone has described the symptom and the layout of the house.

The better question is what you can do before the call to make the search shorter. Read your meter, close fixtures one at a time and note where the water shows up. Every clue you pass along narrows the plumber's job, and a narrower job generally costs less.

Five things that move the price of a leak search

Quotes for finding a leak usually reflect these factors in some combination. Ask your plumber which of them applies to your house.

Where the leak could beA visible stain on a ceiling gives the search a starting point. A high bill with a dry house gives it none, which means testing the system in sections.
Access to the pipesPipes in an open garage wall or under a sink are quick to inspect. Lines in a finished wall or under a concrete floor take more time and more care.
Which method the search needsListening for leak noise, thermal imaging, tracer gas and pipe locating each suit different situations. A search that needs several methods takes longer than one that needs a single test.
Size and layout of the propertyMore bathrooms, more outdoor hose bibs, an irrigation system or a detached structure all add lines to test.
Whether repair is includedSome visits end with a location and a written finding. Others continue straight into opening a wall or digging a trench, which is a separate scope and a separate price.

Insurance can complicate this. Some homeowner policies cover the damage a leak causes but not the cost of finding it, and others treat access to the pipe differently. Read your policy or call your insurer before authorizing work.

Which detection method fits which situation

Each method answers a different question. These are the approaches that plumbing and utility references describe for finding leaks.

MethodWhat it doesBest for
Meter and pressure checkShows whether water is leaving the systemFirst step on any high-bill complaint
Acoustic listening and correlationCompares leak noise at two points on a pipePressurized lines that are buried or in walls
Thermal imagingShows temperature patterns that can mark wet or hot-water leaksHot-water lines and wet building materials
Tracer gasInjects gas and detects where it escapesSmall leaks that make little noise
Pipe locatingTraces where a line actually runsPlanning a dig or an access cut

A good plumber explains which of these they will use and why. They should not offer one method as the answer to every leak.

Do this before the plumber arrives

These checks are safe for any homeowner and they cost nothing. They can also save time on site.

  1. Run the meter testEPA suggests reading the meter, waiting two hours with no water in use, and reading it again. If the reading changed, you probably have a leak.
  2. Check the toilets with dyeA few drops of food coloring in each tank, without flushing, will show up in the bowl if the flapper is leaking. This is a common source of a bill that creeps up.
  3. Look outsideInspect hose bibs, the irrigation valves and the ground around the meter box for wet soil or a hiss. Note any patch of lawn that is greener or softer than the rest.
  4. Note the patternWrite down whether the meter moves only when a certain fixture is used, only at night or all the time. That pattern tells the plumber which part of the system to test first.
  5. Shut the main if water is escapingIf you see water pouring from a wall or ceiling, close the main shutoff valve, avoid wet electrical areas and call right away.

Wasco note: why a leak can hide in sandy loam

The USDA describes the Wasco soil series as a well-drained sandy loam on alluvial fans, with moderately rapid permeability. Water from a leaking buried line can drain away through that kind of soil instead of pooling at the surface.

That is one reason a high water bill can be the only sign of an outdoor leak here. It is also why the meter test matters, and why the City of Wasco's rule that leaks be fixed is worth taking seriously even when the yard looks dry.

Reading a quote so you can compare two of them

A useful quote names what will be tested, what the finding will look like and what happens if the leak is found. Ask whether the price covers locating only, or locating plus the access repair. Ask what happens to the price if the first method finds nothing during a leak detection visit.

If the leak turns out to be a slab leak or a damaged service line, the repair path changes. Our pages on slab leak repair and water service line repair describe those jobs, and you can compare them with the detection step you were quoted.

Slab testing may come up for some homes. Shafter's plumbing code amendment bars new water piping inside a slab on grade, with exceptions, so pipe routing can differ from house to house and town to town. We send licensed plumbers to homes in Shafter and McFarland as well as Wasco.

What EPA says leaks add up to

EPA says the average household's leaks can waste more than 9,300 gallons of water every year. According to EPA, you can read your meter before and after two hours with no water in use, and a changed reading means you probably have a leak. A small, steady leak that a high bill hints at is worth checking now, not next season.
