Fixing a Leaking Hose Bib or Outdoor Spigot

A leaking outside faucet, also called a hose bib or spigot, usually drips for one of three reasons: a loose packing nut at the handle, a worn washer at the spout, or a crack in the pipe behind the wall.

Where the water shows up tells you which one it is, and that decides whether you need a wrench or a plumber.

Where the water appears tells you the cause

Turn the hose bib off, dry it with a rag, then turn it on and watch. Match the leak to a row.

Where it leaksLikely causeUsual fix
Around the handle stem when the faucet is onLoose or worn packing nut or washerTighten the nut a quarter turn, or replace the packing
From the spout when the faucet is closedWorn washer or seatReplace the washer, or the faucet if the seat is pitted
From a vent hole at the top when the hose is runningAnti-siphon vacuum breaker, on faucets that have oneReplace the breaker or the faucet
At the wall, where the faucet meets the houseLoose threads or a crack in the pipeReplace the bib, and check the pipe behind it
Inside the wall or the crawlspace, only when the faucet is openPipe split by a past freezeCall a plumber to open and repair the line

The last row surprises people. A freeze can split the pipe behind the faucet without any sign until you open the valve the following spring.

Stopping a drip at the handle

This is the most common outdoor faucet repair, and it takes little equipment.

  1. Shut off the waterClose the interior shutoff to that line if you have one. If not, close the main water valve, then open the outdoor faucet to drain the pipe.
  2. Remove the hoseDisconnect any hose, splitter or sprayer first. A hose left on traps water and strains the faucet.
  3. Try the packing nutFind the nut just behind the handle. Snug it by a quarter turn with a wrench and do not overtighten.
  4. Replace the packing if it still leaksUnscrew the handle, remove the nut, and swap the old packing or washer for a matching new one.
  5. Turn the water back on slowlyOpen the shutoff, then the faucet, and watch the stem. If the leak is gone, you are done.

A drip from the spout means the seat or washer is gone

If water seeps out of the spout with the handle closed, the washer is no longer sealing against the seat. Replacing the washer fixes it if the seat is smooth. A pitted seat keeps leaking, so the whole faucet is the better replacement.

Home inspectors' guidelines list hose bibs as lasting roughly 20 to 30 years. If yours is near that age and the stem is corroded, a new faucet takes less time than repeated patching. Faucet repair often ends this way, since old brass and seals wear together.

When a leaking spigot cannot wait

A drip into a flower bed is a nuisance. Water inside the house is not.

Act right away if you see this

  • !Water spraying behind the wall Shut off the main water valve and keep power away from wet areas, such as outlets near the faucet.
  • !A wet interior wall, ceiling or crawlspace The pipe behind the faucet may have split. Do not wait for it to dry on its own.
  • !The faucet will not shut off Close the main valve and call the 24/7 line. We can arrange same-day service when available.
  • !A spreading wet patch in the yard This can mean a buried line, not the faucet. Call 811 before anyone digs.

Keeping an outside faucet from failing again

Most hose bib failures trace back to hoses left on and cold nights. A few habits prevent both.

  • ✓Unhook hoses before cold weather A connected hose holds water against the faucet and can freeze it solid.
  • ✓Add an insulated faucet cover Foam covers are inexpensive and work well for faucets with no frost protection.
  • ✓Open the faucet slightly after shutting the interior valve This lets the last water drain out of the pipe before a freeze.
  • ✓Look at the faucet each spring Turn it on and check inside the wall or crawlspace for drips.
  • ✓Do not leave the sprayer clipped on Pressure builds against a closed nozzle and loosens the faucet stem over time.
