Toilet leaking, overflowing, or running without stopping

If water is reaching the bathroom floor, call the 24/7 emergency line at any hour and we will send a licensed plumber to your Wasco address. While you wait, turn the small oval valve on the wall behind the toilet clockwise until it stops, and do not flush again.

Toilet repair means finding which part has failed, whether that is the fill valve, the flapper, the wax ring under the bowl, or the porcelain itself, and replacing only what is worn.

Most calls start with something you can see or hear. A hiss after the tank has filled points at the fill valve or flapper. A damp ring around the base points lower, to the seal between the bowl and the floor. A cracked tank or bowl cannot be patched and becomes a replacement.

The plumber confirms the cause before any work is priced. That means closing the supply, drying everything, and watching where water first reappears, because a flapper leak and a wax ring leak can look identical on a wet floor and involve very different amounts of work.

Bowl rising or water on the floor: do this first

These steps take about a minute and limit the damage while a plumber is on the way.

Stop the water, then call

  • !Do not flush again. A second flush on a slow bowl is the usual reason a small overflow becomes a flooded floor. If the water is near the rim, lift the tank lid and press the rubber flapper down to stop more water entering the bowl.
  • !Close the stop valve behind the toilet. Turn it clockwise by hand. If it is stuck, spins freely, or drips from the stem, close the main shutoff for the house instead and tell the dispatcher, since a worn stop valve is a separate repair.
  • !Keep power away from the wet area. Unplug anything on the floor, stay off wet surfaces near outlets, and switch off the breaker for that bathroom if water has reached a receptacle or a floor heater.
  • !Check the other drains. If the tub or sink gurgles when the toilet drains, the blockage may be in the main line and drain cleaning is the right call, not a toilet part. Stop using all water until it is cleared.
  • !Take photos before you mop. Pictures of the floor, the toilet and any stains on the ceiling below help if you file a claim later.

Why a running toilet is a bill problem and a city rule

A leaking flapper lets water slide from the tank into the bowl all day, often silently. To check, read your water meter, wait two hours with no water in use, and read it again. EPA lists worn toilet flappers among the most common household leaks, and a meter that moved means water went somewhere.

The City of Wasco's water conservation chapter requires leaks to be fixed and treats 48 hours after discovery as a reasonable window. That makes a quietly running toilet worth repairing this week, not next season.

Rubber flappers and fill valve seals soften in treated water, and the city's 2022 water quality report lists a chlorine residual averaging 0.67 ppm in its system. If the toilet is quiet but the meter still moves after you have ruled it out, leak detection looks for the loss elsewhere in the house.

Wax rings, flanges, and the leak at the base

The wax ring seals the toilet outlet to the drain flange in the floor. When the seal fails, water seeps out on every flush and soaks the subfloor, and the first sign is often a stain below or a floor that feels spongy near the bowl. Caulk around the base does not fix it and can hide the evidence, so leave the gap open until the cause is known.

A new ring only holds if the flange is sound and sits at the right height. A cracked, corroded or loose flange needs a repair ring or a replacement first. If the subfloor has gone soft, the plumber will tell you before any new toilet is set, because a fresh seal on rotten wood fails again.

Signs a new toilet beats another repair

Repairing makes sense when one part is the problem. These signs point to replacing the fixture.

  • ✓A crack in the bowl or tank. Porcelain does not seal back up, and a tank crack can let go at the worst time.
  • ✓Repeated visits for the same leak. When a flapper, a fill valve and a ring have all been changed, the remaining problem is usually the body of the toilet.
  • ✓Weak flush and frequent clogs. An old bowl with a worn flush passage may keep clogging even though the drain line is clear.
  • ✓Parts that are hard to match. Older tanks sometimes need parts that no longer fit modern valves.
  • ✓A remodel is already planned. A new vanity or floor is the least disruptive moment to replace a tired toilet.

If you are unsure which side of the line you are on, ask the plumber to price both options and show you the failed part.

Choosing a replacement: flush rating, height, and fit

California sets toilets at 1.28 gallons per flush in CALGreen, so a new residential toilet should carry that rating on the box or tank. A lower rating on its own does not guarantee a strong flush, so compare how each model clears the bowl, not only the number printed on it.

Comfort height bowls sit taller than standard ones and are easier on knees and backs for many adults, though shorter users and small children may prefer a standard seat. Elongated bowls need more room than round ones. The rough-in, the distance from the finished wall to the center of the floor drain, must also match the new model or the toilet will not sit square.

Moving a toilet to a different spot in the room is a piping job, not a swap, and belongs under plumbing installation. Whether a fixture swap needs a permit is set by the city, so ask the City of Wasco before you buy.

How a replacement visit runs

A like-for-like swap moves in a predictable order.

  1. Measure and inspectThe plumber checks the rough-in, the flange and the floor around it before the old toilet is touched.
  2. Shut off and drainThe supply is closed, the tank and bowl are emptied, and the supply line is disconnected.
  3. Lift out the old unitThe old wax is scraped clean and the flange is checked for cracks, rust and height.
  4. Set the new toiletA new ring and bolts go in, the bowl is seated straight, and a new braided supply line is connected.
  5. Test before leavingThe toilet is flushed repeatedly, the base and connections are checked for seepage, and the stop valve is tried so you know it still works.
