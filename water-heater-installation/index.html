Hot water that runs out: where an installation plan starts

When a household drains the tank before the last shower, the first thing to check is demand against capacity. More people, a soaking tub or a second bathroom added since the heater was set can leave a tank too small for the way the house is used today. Water heater installation in Wasco, CA is the planned work of selecting, placing, connecting, venting and testing a new heater so it matches the home.

That is different from a failure call. If your heater has already given out, use the page on replacing a failed tank instead. This page is for people who have time to choose well, including remodelers, builders and owners upgrading before something breaks.

Sizing a heater to the household

Tank capacity and recovery rate work together. A large tank stores more hot water, while a fast-recovery burner or element refills it sooner. Households with simultaneous demand, such as laundry running during showers, need more of one or both.

Start with the real pattern of use, not a rule of thumb. How many people shower in the same morning window, and does anyone fill a large tub? Does a dishwasher or washer draw hot water at the same time? Those answers shape the capacity, and the plumber confirms them against the space the heater has to fit.

Venting, gas line and electrical: the hookups behind the unit

These connections decide whether a new heater works safely. Most surprises in an install come from one of them.

  • ✓Venting A gas heater has a flue or vent that carries combustion gases outside. Size, slope, material and clearance follow the maker's instructions and code, and a new unit may not use the old vent unchanged.
  • ✓Gas line The line has to supply enough gas and end at an accessible shutoff with a sound connector. Gas work is within the plumbing license (C-36 covers gas appliance connections), and new piping is pressure tested before use.
  • ✓Electrical An electric tank needs a dedicated circuit sized to the unit, and a heat pump model may need its own provisions. Panel capacity is checked before the order.
  • ✓Water connections Shutoff valves and flexible connectors are inspected, and old corroded ones are replaced, not reused by default.

For homes with SoCalGas service, which includes Wasco, Shafter and McFarland, the gas company is the right contact for any smell of gas. Leave the building first and call from outside.

Heat pump models and the Energy Code

The 2025 Energy Code, in effect since January 1, 2026, encourages heat pump water heaters in new homes. Gas water heating in new dwellings must reserve space and provisions for a future heat pump unit. Heat pump models need backup heat if their inlet air is unconditioned.

Code items every new tank needs

California law requires water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake movement (Health and Safety Code 19211), and that includes new installations. The relief valve discharge pipe stays open, unthreaded at the end and never capped.

A drain pan under a heater in a finished space, or above living areas, catches slow leaks and can be piped to a safe discharge. A thermal expansion tank gives heated water room to expand in a closed system, and the plumber confirms whether your plumbing needs one. Where water pressure is above 80 psi, the California Plumbing Code calls for a pressure regulator.

Moving a heater or building new

Relocating a heater usually means new supply and hot lines, a new vent path, and a fresh gas or electric run. It is worth doing when the old spot blocks a remodel, crowds a garage door, or sits far from the kitchen and baths.

In new construction, the heater location can be fixed on the plans, before the walls close. That is the easiest time to leave a vent path, a condensate drain, a gas stub or a dedicated circuit in place. If your project also includes a layout of fixtures and piping, plumbing installation covers that side of the work.
